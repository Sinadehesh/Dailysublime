SARAH: [Quietly] Delaying the sentence...

MARCUS: Reading the gradients before you land on a verdict. Light, for Vermeer, didn't explain things. It revealed relationships. And that distinction is everything.

SARAH: Okay...

MARCUS: That highlight is why the face is alive. The pearl shows you Vermeer can paint luxury. The lip glint shows you he understood presence. The softness of a breath. The second before someone speaks. If you miss it, you've looked at the painting. If you find it, you've met her.

SARAH: [Quietly stunned] I have never in my life thought about a lip highlight as a philosophical statement.

It bleeds into the background. He's saying: the loudest thing in the room still has a soft edge. Your eye wants to stop at the red, but the painting won't let you be lazy.

SARAH: [Mind blown] He's literally fighting your attention span from inside a canvas.

MARCUS: From 1665. With oil paint. Pre-internet.

MARCUS: Empty pans. Almost stillness. And behind her, on the wall — a painting of the Last Judgment. All that cosmic drama, all that divine reckoning in the background — and she's just quietly holding an almost-still scale. Not panicking. Not rushing.

SARAH: He keeps doing that. Saying wait.

MARCUS: That is his entire philosophy. In a world that keeps saying go.
